# Aisíoc

**Get the tax back you're owed.** A free site that shows you which Irish tax credits you're missing and how to claim them yourself.

👉 **https://aisioc.github.io/**

---

Hi all,

Bit of a story. I knew about the rent tax credit but never knew how to claim it. A colleague of mine walked me through the steps. I've been renting for years, and turns out I'd been leaving money on the table. From talking to people, I'm definitely not the only one. Revenue's own figures say over 500,000 people didn't claim refunds they were due in 2024.

So I built a site to help: **Aisíoc** (Irish for refund). You answer a few quick questions, takes about 2 minutes, and it tells you which tax credits you're probably missing, roughly what they're worth, and the exact steps to claim each one yourself on myAccount.

It covers stuff like:

- Rent Tax Credit (up to €1,000 a year)
- Medical expenses (GP, prescriptions, non-routine dental)
- Flat rate expenses for nurses, teachers, shop workers, builders and loads more
- Working from home relief
- Emergency tax refunds
- Tuition fees, mortgage interest, single parent and carer credits, and a few others

**Heads up:** you can only claim back 4 years, so claims for **2022 close on 31 December 2026**. If you were renting in 2022, that's up to €500 gone if you don't claim it.

A few things:

- It's completely free. No ads, no sign-up, and I'm not selling anyone's data.
- Your answers aren't saved anywhere. It never asks for your PPS number or myAccount password (and nobody legit will, so watch out for refund scam texts).
- You don't need to pay someone a cut to do this for you. It's about 15 minutes per year in myAccount.
- I'm not connected to Revenue and it's not tax advice. I've checked everything against Revenue.ie, but if you spot a mistake there's a feedback page and I'll fix it.

There's also a **Benefits & Support** page for stuff that isn't tax but is still your money. Things like the Drug Payment Scheme (medicines capped at €80 a month), free dental check-ups through PRSI, GP visit cards and the Carer's Support Grant. Loads of people don't know about these either.

## What's coming next

Once I started building this I realised tax isn't the only thing people get stuck on. There's a load of stuff in Ireland where you either don't know what you're entitled to, or the info is spread across five different websites. So I'm going to keep adding small tools under Aisíoc, all working the same way: free, nothing saved, no sign-up.

Here's what's on the list:

- **Payslip checker.** Put in your pay and see if the right tax, USC and PRSI is coming out. If you're on emergency tax or missing a credit, you'll know straight away instead of finding out years later.
- **Redundancy calculator.** If you're losing your job, work out your redundancy pay, notice and any holidays you're owed. It's a stressful time and the last thing you need is to be guessing.
- **Renters' rights.** Is your rent increase actually legal? How much notice does your landlord have to give you? The rules have changed a few times so a lot of people don't know where they stand. I'm renting myself so this one is close to home.
- **First-time buyer.** How much you can borrow and what Help to Buy and the First Home scheme could add on top. Buying a house here is hard enough without the maths.
- **Childcare subsidy.** What the National Childcare Scheme takes off your crèche bill, so parents know before they sign up, not after.

Why keep them all under the one site? Because it's easier to trust one name than a pile of random sites, and you can find everything in one place from the Tools menu. Each tool will only go live once I've checked it properly against the official sources (Revenue, the RTB, Citizens Information, the Central Bank).

I don't want to guess which one people need most, so there's a **Survey** on the site. Tick the ones you'd use and I'll build the most popular first. It's anonymous and takes about 10 seconds. If there's something that's not on the list, there's a box in the survey to suggest it.

Hope it gets a few of you some money back. Sláinte
